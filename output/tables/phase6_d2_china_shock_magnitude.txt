Reading -- the two shocks have very different distributional shapes:

  Carbon shock (Phase IV, annual, all Belgian buyers):
    Narrow but deep. Most buyers (p50, p75, p90) have ZERO ETS exposure.
    Concentrated at the top tail: p99 = 6.90%, p95 = 0.031%, mean = 0.358%.
    The ~1%% of buyers in cement/steel/refining bear most of the burden.

  China shock (B2B-side, 10-yr LR, all Belgian buyers):
    Broad but shallow. Almost every buyer has some exposure via their
    NACE-4d input mix; few buyers have very large exposure.
    p99 = 1.13%, p95 = 0.448%, mean = 0.149%.
    Annualized (LR / 10): p99 = 0.113%, p95 = 0.0448%, mean = 0.0149%.

  Headline ratios (where both shocks are non-zero):
    p95 (cumulative): China is 14.6x carbon
    p95 (annualized): China is 1.46x carbon
    mean (cumulative): China is 0.42x carbon
    mean (annualized): China is 0.042x carbon
    p99 (cumulative): China is 0.16x carbon (carbon dominates the tail)

  Bottom line:
    - For the median Belgian buyer: China is bigger (carbon = 0).
    - For the typical exposed buyer (p95): China and carbon are
      comparable on annualized basis.
    - For the high-exposure tail (p99): carbon is far bigger because
      it concentrates on a small set of regulated emitters.
    - The two shocks tag different firms: cement/steel for carbon,
      manufacturers with import-substitutable inputs for China.

Caveats:
  - B2B-side only (Customs-side omitted; would shift China shock up).
  - B2B is downsampled on local-1; tail moments will refine on RMD.
  - Horizon mismatch: China is 10-yr LR cumulative, carbon is Phase IV
    annual. Annualized ratio divides China by 10 for per-year speed.
